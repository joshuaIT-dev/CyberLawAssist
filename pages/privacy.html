Privacy Policy

1. Scope of this policy

This policy explains how the current CyberLawAssist system handles information for Guests, Users, and office administrators. It covers the website, local chatbot, Live support, checklists, account management, and physical intake records stored through the office workspace.

The office contact below is the contact point for questions about information handled through this project. External websites have their own privacy notices.

2. Information the system handles

  • Accounts: name, email, optional phone number, role, account status, preferences, and, for administrators, an admin number. Passwords are stored as hashes rather than readable passwords.
  • New User registration: the policy version and time when the Terms were accepted and the Privacy Policy acknowledged.
  • Chatbot and checklists: questions, replies, conversation titles, pin status, item text, completion status, and associated timestamps.
  • Live support: messages, the User's contact details, the replying administrator's role, notifications, and read markers.
  • Office intake: visitor name, contact number when supplied, concern category, visit date, assigned staff, status, and factual notes entered by an administrator.
  • Technical information: session identifiers, sign-in failure counts and lockout times, and request information that the local server may record in operational logs.

A description of a concern can contain sensitive information. Share only what is needed. Do not provide passwords, one-time codes, recovery codes, or unnecessary sensitive documents.

3. Why information is used

Information is used to identify your account, maintain the correct workspace access, retrieve chatbot answers, save requested activity, organize checklists, support office conversations, record office intake, produce activity reports, and protect account access.

The current application has no configured advertising system, marketing distribution service, cloud AI provider, or external email/SMS notification service. In-app notifications relate to activity in the system.

4. Local chatbot processing

Chatbot questions pass through the website's PHP service to the local Python service. The current bot retrieves content from local question-and-answer files. It does not send questions to an external AI provider or automatically train itself on conversations.

Guest questions are processed locally, while Guest conversation history is saved in the browser rather than the account database. User and Admin chatbot histories are saved under their own account IDs in the database.

5. Who can access information

Users can access their own account records. Administrators can see registered-user directory details, office intake records, and Live support conversations so they can reply and manage the service. Admin access does not include an application feature for browsing another account's private AI history.

People authorized to administer the host computer, database, or backups may have technical access to stored data. Access through those tools must be managed by the office. Applicable legal duties may also require an assessment of a request for disclosure; using this site does not make all submitted information public.

6. Sessions, local storage, and file caching

  • A session cookie keeps your login associated with the browser. Ordinary tabs in the same browser profile share the session.
  • Guest chat history and Guest preferences remain in local browser storage until deleted or browser storage is cleared. Other people using the same browser profile may be able to open that Guest history.
  • The browser may remember interface preferences and the selected conversation. Account records remain on the server.
  • The application cache stores only selected public policy pages, an offline notice, and static interface files. It does not cache API responses, passwords, signed-in workspace pages, private conversations, Live support data, or exported documents.

You can clear the public file cache below. This does not delete Guest history or account records; use History controls or contact the office for those records.

7. External links and icon files

The existing icon library is loaded from an external CDN when available. That request can expose normal connection information, such as the requesting device's IP address, to the provider. The icon request does not intentionally include chatbot or account content.

Opening an external law or agency link takes you to another website with its own privacy practices. No external website is given access to your CyberLawAssist account merely because its link appears here.

8. Retention, deletion, and downloaded copies

The application does not currently enforce an automatic deletion schedule. Saved account activity remains until it is removed using available controls or by an authorized administrator. Guest local history remains until removed in the browser.

You can delete your own AI conversations. Administrators can delete User accounts and their linked personal chat, checklist, support, and notification records after confirmation. Office intake records and records linked to an office record creator may need separate handling.

Removing a live record does not automatically erase an existing database backup, printed copy, or downloaded Word document. Contact the office to ask about its recordkeeping obligations, backup handling, and the retention period that applies to a particular record.

9. Account and device security

The application uses password hashing, session cookies, request validation, role checks, and ownership checks. No security control guarantees that a device or record can never be compromised. The current localhost connection uses HTTP; transport encryption depends on how the host is deployed.

Use a private device where possible, keep your password confidential, log out on shared devices, and secure exported files. Report suspected account misuse to the office. Do not enter unrelated sensitive details merely because a form or message box accepts text.

10. Privacy requests and rights

Under Philippine privacy rules, you may ask for information about processing, request access to your personal information, seek corrections, or request restriction or removal where the applicable conditions allow. You may also raise a concern with the National Privacy Commission. These rights can have legal limits, including applicable recordkeeping duties.

Contact the office using the details below, describe the record or concern, and ask how to submit the request securely. The office may need to verify your identity before releasing, changing, or deleting records. Do not send your password or one-time code as proof of identity.

References: National Privacy Commission: data subject rights and Data Privacy Act of 2012.
